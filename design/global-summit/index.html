Project context

What existed before

  • Meeting requests arrived through calls and messaging apps.
  • Schedules and assignments were tracked in spreadsheets.
  • Status changes were communicated person to person.

Information was fragmented across channels, so no one had a reliable picture of what was actually scheduled or who was responsible.

What triggered the work

A large-scale international summit where multiple principals, meetings, schedules, assignments and teams had to be coordinated at the same time.

User flow

How the product works

Core journey

  1. 1A meeting request is raised for a principal.
  2. 2Availability is checked against existing commitments.
  3. 3A liaison officer is assigned and confirms the request.
  4. 4The schedule updates and affected teams are alerted.
  5. 5Status is tracked through to completion and reporting.

What was simplified

  • Meeting requests
  • Schedules
  • Assignments
  • Alerts
  • Meeting statuses

The main structural decision was to make the schedule the single source of truth, with requests, assignments and alerts all resolving into it.

User experience & trust

How the experience should feel

Before

  • Uncertainty about whether a change had reached everyone
  • Pressure from high-stakes, time-critical meetings
  • Low confidence in the accuracy of shared documents

Experience goals

CalmIn controlConfident in the current stateQuick to scan

Dense but quiet information design: clear status language, visible ownership on every record, and alerts tied to the exact meeting that changed.

User behaviour

How the design supports user behaviour

  1. 1Coordinator checks the schedule for the day
  2. 2Acts on requests, conflicts and assignment gaps
  3. 3System reflects the change immediately
  4. 4Stakeholders see an accurate view and stop re-confirming by phone

The behaviour the product tries to change is the habit of verifying information over calls and chats because the shared record is not trusted.

Key decisions

The choices that shaped the product

01

One schedule, many entry points

Rather than separate tools for requests, assignments and calendars, every action resolves into a single schedule so there is one version of the truth.

02

Availability shown at the point of request

Conflicts were the most expensive failure, so availability context appears while a request is being made, not after it is submitted.

03

Status as plain language

Meeting states use operational language the teams already used, which reduced the amount of training needed during a fast-moving event.

04

AI-assisted wireframes for stakeholder alignment

Early low-fidelity direction was produced quickly so stakeholders could react to something concrete before detailed design began.

Expected Impact

Proposed success targets to validate after launch; these are not measured outcomes.

Reduce reliance on manual tools

Target: replace 70–80% of WhatsApp messages, calls, and spreadsheet updates with structured in-platform actions.

Improve visibility

Target: reduce the CLO’s time to assess full-team status from an estimated 15–20 minutes to under 2 minutes through a single dashboard.

STAR summary

The project in four parts

situation

The event team was coordinating high-stakes meetings between international principals and liaison officers across multiple stakeholders, making coordination difficult to track.

task

Create a centralized platform that simplified meeting coordination, assignments, scheduling and operational visibility.

action

Designed the platform end-to-end across principal management, liaison workflows, meetings, scheduling, alerts and reporting. AI-assisted wireframes were used to validate direction with stakeholders before moving into final design.

result

Created a scalable coordination system that brought important operational workflows into one place and improved visibility across principals, liaison officers, meetings and event operations.

Reflection

What I learned

  • In operations products, trust in the data matters more than interface novelty — the design work was mostly about making the record believable.
  • Getting a rough structure in front of stakeholders early removed weeks of debate about scope.

Future scope

What I would validate next

  • Validate coordination time and conflict rates with real event data.
  • Templates so the platform can be reused across future summits.
  • Role-based reporting for organising leadership.
